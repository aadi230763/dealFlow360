**This file supersedes IMPLEMENTATION.md. Work only from this file.**

Current state as of V2:
- Frontend has been rebuilt against the organizers' 18-screen mockup. Navigation, Sales Dashboard, Quotations List (Kanban + table toggle), Quotation Detail, Approvals List, Approval Detail, and the Products screens are done.

What remains: Phase 4 through Phase 9.

---

## Rules for the coding agent

1. Work one phase at a time. At the end of each, print the **Verification Gate** and stop. Do not begin the next phase until the human signs off.
2. Every phase replaces a placeholder screen with a working one. No phase is backend-only.
3. Business rules are database rows, never constants in code. If you are about to write a hardcoded percentage or threshold, stop and make it configuration.
4. Never fake a computation. Every number on screen comes from an engine function.
5. Do not modify anything in `/engine/pricing.py`, `/engine/risk.py`, or `/engine/routing.py`. They are verified and the demo depends on them.
6. Do not rework completed frontend screens. If a new phase needs a change to an existing screen, make the smallest possible change and note it.
7. Append to `PROGRESS.md` after each phase: what was built, what was skipped, known breakage, and any judgment call you made.
8. Every state change writes an audit event. No exceptions.

## Rules for the human

At each gate, click through it yourself. "It's implemented" is not a pass. A gate failure at Phase 5 is thirty minutes; the same failure discovered at Phase 9 is your demo.

---

## The pitch (keep in view)

Most sales tools are a quote-to-invoice form. DealFlow360 governs itself: it enforces pricing discipline per product category, routes approvals without anyone asking, reacts to live inventory, reconciles subscriptions against one-time hardware on one order, and gives the customer a live negotiable document instead of a PDF.

**The moment we win on:** a judge changes a discount ceiling on the config screen, we recompute, and an already-approved quotation flips back into the approval queue with an audit entry showing old score versus new. Everything below protects that moment.

# PHASE 4 — Upsell and cross-sell
**Target: 1 hour**

Short, high demo value. The visible "the system is helping me" beat.

## Backend
- `engine/upsell.py` → `suggest(quotation, pairings, products, min_margin) -> [Suggestion]`.
  Rank by `co_purchase_score`, boost products flagged `is_promoted`, and **exclude anything whose margin falls below the pairing's `min_margin_pct`** — we do not suggest deals that hurt us.
  Each suggestion's margin delta and new order total come from calling `price_quotation` on the hypothetical line set. No estimates, no approximations.
- `GET /api/quotations/{id}/suggestions`
- `POST /api/quotations/{id}/suggestions/{product_id}/dismiss` — dismissed items don't return in the session.

## Frontend — screen 4, existing page
Add the **"Upsell and Cross-Sell Suggestions"** section *below* the order lines table, as drawn. A row of three cards. Each card heads with "+ Product name" and shows either a margin figure ("Margin +$18") or a promo tag ("Promo: 12% off"). Clicking adds the line; a small dismiss control sits in the corner.

On add: the line appears in the table, totals and margin update, the Limit/Status cells evaluate, and the risk band re-computes. The whole chain reacting to one click is the point of this phase.

## Verification Gate 4
- [ ] Adding a laptop surfaces relevant accessories, not random products.
- [ ] Promoted items rank above equal-scored non-promoted ones.
- [ ] Lowering a product's price in admin until its margin drops below the pairing floor removes it from suggestions.
- [ ] The stated margin delta exactly matches the actual margin change after adding.
- [ ] Adding a suggestion can push the quote across a threshold, and the risk band changes immediately.
- [ ] Dismiss removes it for the session.

# PHASE 5 — Multi-warehouse fulfillment
**Target: 2.5 hours**

## Backend

Models:
- `Fulfillment(id, quotation_id, status, total_shipments, estimated_cost, is_manual_override, created_at)`
- `FulfillmentAllocation(id, fulfillment_id, quotation_line_id, warehouse_id, qty, is_backorder, shipped_at)`

`shipped_at` is load-bearing — Phase 6 invoices off it. Do not omit it.

`engine/fulfillment.py` → `plan_split(lines, stock_levels, warehouses) -> FulfillmentPlan`

Objective, in this order: **minimize shipment count first, then weighted shipping cost.** Satisfy the order from the fewest warehouses possible; prefer warehouses that can cover the most lines completely; break ties on `shipping_cost_weight`. Anything unsatisfiable becomes a backorder allocation. Return `explanations[]` per line — "12 of 20 from Main Warehouse (all available), 8 backordered."

Endpoints:
- `POST /api/quotations/{id}/fulfillment/plan` — plan only, no writes
- `POST /api/quotations/{id}/fulfillment/accept` — persists and reserves stock (`StockLevel.reserved += qty`)
- `POST /api/fulfillment/{id}/override` — accepts a manual allocation set, **validates against available stock**, rejects impossible allocations with a readable error
- `POST /api/fulfillment/{id}/consolidate` — re-plans open backorders when stock has arrived
- `POST /api/fulfillment/allocations/{id}/ship` — sets `shipped_at`. **This is the trigger Phase 6 hangs off.** Build it now even though nothing consumes it yet.
- `PATCH /api/stock/{id}` — admin edits on-hand from screen 7

Planning runs automatically when a quotation reaches `APPROVED`.

## Frontend

**Screen 7 — Fulfillment and Stock (List).** Subtitle: "Live stock per warehouse, plus every order that still needs fulfilling." Two stacked tables:
- *Stock* — Warehouse, Product, **In Stock, Reserved, Available**. On-hand is editable inline for admins; this is the only warehouse admin surface in the mockup.
- *Orders Awaiting Fulfillment* — Order, Customer, Status (Split Pending / Backorder), Warehouse ("Main + East Depot").
- Note strip: "Click an order row to open its warehouse split detail."

**Screen 8 — Fulfillment Detail**, titled "Fulfillment Detail: Q-1042 (Acme Corp)". Table columns **Warehouse, Qty Fulfilled, Est. Shipments, Cost** — rows like "Main Warehouse / 18 units / 1 / $12", "East Depot / 6 units / 1 / $29". Yellow prompt below: "'Consolidate Remaining Backorder' prompt appears automatically once East Depot restocks." Footer: **Accept Suggested Split** (primary) and **Manual Override** (secondary). Override mode makes qty-per-warehouse editable with live validation against Available.

Add a per-allocation **Ship** action and an expandable explanation row under each warehouse. The explanation is what proves the split is computed rather than hardcoded.

## Verification Gate 5

- [ ] An order exceeding any single warehouse's stock splits across two, and quantities sum exactly to the ordered quantity.
- [ ] An order exceeding total stock creates a backorder row with the correct shortfall.

# PHASE 6 — Hybrid billing
**Target: 2.5 hours**

## The rule that changed

**Mockup screen 13 states: "Partial invoicing stays reconciled with partial delivery, nothing is billed before it ships."**

One-time lines are **not** invoiced on order confirmation. They are invoiced when their allocation ships. A split order shipping 18 of 24 units invoices 18 now and 6 when the backorder ships. Recurring lines are invoiced at the start of their period regardless of shipment, per screen 17's note.

This couples Phase 6 to Phase 5. Implement it correctly the first time.

## Backend

Models:
- `Order(id, quotation_id, number, status, confirmed_at)`
- `Invoice(id, order_id, type, amount, tax, status, issue_date, due_date, period_start, period_end)` — `type` in {ONE_TIME, RECURRING}, `status` in {DRAFT, ISSUED, PAID, PARTIAL, CREDITED}
- `BillingSchedule(id, order_id, quotation_line_id, plan_id, next_billing_date, interval, amount, status)` — status in {ACTIVE, PAUSED, CANCELLED}
- `Payment(id, invoice_id, amount, method, reference, received_at)`
- `CreditNote(id, invoice_id, amount, reason, created_at)`

`engine/billing.py`:
- `build_schedule(order)` — recurring lines produce a schedule of the next 12 periods with dates and amounts, first charge at period start. One-time lines produce pending invoice *intents* that materialize on shipment.
- `invoice_shipment(allocation)` — creates or extends the one-time invoice for the units that actually shipped. Called from the ship endpoint built in Phase 5.
- `prorate(line, change_date, old_qty, new_qty, plan)` — daily proration by default: `delta_amount × (days_remaining / days_in_period)`. Increase → prorated charge; decrease → prorated credit note. Honors the plan's `proration_policy`.
- `cancel(subscription, date, policy)` → refund or credit per policy.

Endpoints:
- `POST /api/quotations/{id}/confirm` → creates order and recurring schedules
- `GET /api/subscriptions` → global, across all customers and orders
- `GET /api/invoices` and `GET /api/invoices/{id}`
- `POST /api/invoices/{id}/payments` → records payment, sets PAID or PARTIAL
- `POST /api/subscriptions/{id}/change` → applies proration, emits credit note or charge
- `POST /api/subscriptions/{id}/cancel`

## Frontend — four screens, two nav entries

**Screen 9 — Subscriptions List.** Subtitle: "Every recurring plan across every customer, regardless of which order it came from." **Global**, not per-order. Count chips: Active / Paused / Cancelled. Table: Customer, Plan, Cycle, Next Bill, Status. Footer: "+ New Plan (Admin)" — subscription plans are created here, admin-only.

**Screen 10 — Billing Detail**, titled "Billing Detail: Acme Corp — Care Plan 2yr". Two labelled tables: *One-Time Lines (from originating order)* — Product, Qty, Amount; *Recurring Lines* — Plan, Cycle, Next Bill Date, Amount. Footer: **Modify Subscription** and **Cancel Subscription** (red outline). Modify opens a proration preview before confirming: "Increasing 5 → 8 seats with 14 of 30 days remaining: charge $X now, then $Y per month."

**Screen 12 — Invoices List.** Subtitle: "Every invoice generated from one-time and recurring lines." Count chips: Unpaid / Paid. Table: Invoice #, Customer, Amount, Status, Due Date, with recurring invoices marked. Note strip: "Click an invoice row to open its full payment and delivery reconciliation detail."

**Screen 13 — Invoice Detail.** Top: horizontal stepper **Order Confirmed → Shipped → Invoiced → Paid**, reflecting this order's real stage. Table of invoice rows including recurring ones ("INV-1043 (Recurring)"). Footer: **Record Payment** (green) and **Download Summary**. Note strip: "Partial invoicing stays reconciled with partial delivery, nothing is billed before it ships."

# PHASE 7 — Customer portal and negotiation
**Target: 2.5 hours**

The spec explicitly requires this be a genuinely separate restricted view, not an internal screen relabelled. Build the isolation properly and say so in the pitch.

## Backend
- `PortalToken(id, quotation_id, customer_id, token_hash, expires_at, used_at)` — signed, `aud: "portal"`, scoped to **one** quotation.
- All portal routes under `/api/portal/*` with their own dependency. A portal token authenticates to exactly one quotation ID; any other returns **404, not 403** — do not leak existence.
- **A separate Pydantic response schema.** No cost, no margin, no risk, no internal comments. Do not filter an internal schema — build a new one, or something will leak.
- `NegotiationRequest(id, quotation_id, line_id nullable, type, message, proposed_discount_pct, requested_delivery_date, status, created_at, responded_at, responder_user_id, response_message)` — type in {COMMENT, CHANGE_REQUEST, COUNTER_DISCOUNT}.

Endpoints:
- `POST /api/quotations/{id}/send` — generates token and shareable link, sets status `SENT`
- `GET /api/portal/quotation`
- `POST /api/portal/negotiate` — sets `UNDER_NEGOTIATION`, notifies the rep by SSE
- `POST /api/portal/confirm` — **the automatic re-entry rule:** re-price and re-run risk against final agreed terms. If a chain is now required, status returns to `PENDING_APPROVAL` and the customer sees "Sent for internal review." If not, status becomes `CONFIRMED` and fulfillment planning runs.
- `POST /api/negotiations/{id}/respond` — rep accepts, counters or declines; accepting applies the discount and re-runs risk

## Frontend

**Screen 11 — Customer Portal Negotiation.** A separate route tree at `/portal/:token` with its **own navigation — My Quotation · Messages · Profile** — and none of the internal nav.
- Subtitle: "Customer reviews and negotiates the quote directly, no email needed."
- Prominent status chip: Sent / **Under Negotiation** / Confirmed.
- Table with columns **Line** and **Customer Comment**, one row per quotation line, comment editable inline. Mockup examples: "Can this be 15% off instead of 10%?", "Can we push this to next month?"
- Two fields below: **Counter Discount %** and **Requested Delivery Date**.
- Footer: **Submit Request** (secondary) and **Confirm Quotation** (green primary).
- Note strip: "If final terms exceed thresholds, the quote automatically re-enters approval (Screen 6)."

Internal side: a negotiation inbox on the Quotation Detail, threaded, with accept / counter / decline. Accepting a counter visibly moves the risk band.

## Verification Gate 7
- [ ] The portal link opens in a private window with no login and shows the right quotation.
- [ ] **Inspect the raw JSON in the network tab** — no cost, margin or risk fields anywhere. Check the payload, not the UI.
- [ ] Changing the quotation ID in the portal URL returns 404.

- [ ] A customer counter appears in the rep's inbox live, without refresh.
- [ ] **Key check:** customer counters above threshold, rep accepts, customer confirms → the quote automatically re-enters approval with the correct chain. Nobody clicked "request approval."

# PHASE 8 — Deal health, anomalies and reporting
**Target: 2 hours**

Everything here is a read over data we already have. No new sources of truth.

## Backend

`engine/anomaly.py`:
- **Stalled deals** — non-terminal quotations with `last_activity_at` older than the configured threshold, ranked by value at risk.
- **Discount anomaly** — per rep, rolling mean and standard deviation of order-level discount % across their history. Flag where `z = (discount − mean) / stddev` exceeds the configured threshold. Fall back to a fixed-delta rule when a rep has too few historical quotes.
- **Delivery slippage** — fulfillments with open backorders past their promised date.

Endpoints:
- `GET /api/dashboard/health` — three alert lists with drill-through IDs
- `GET /api/dashboard/metrics` — quotes created, average approval time from real approval timestamps, top upsold product
- `GET /api/reports` — filters: period, sales team/rep, approval status, product/category
- `POST /api/quotations/{id}/nudge` and `POST /api/quotations/{id}/escalate` — both write audit entries
- Quotation PDF export, report CSV export

## Frontend

**Screen 14 — Deal Health and Anomaly Dashboard.** Subtitle: "Real-time flags for stalled deals and unusual discount patterns." Three cards: **Stalled Deals** ("5 quotes idle 7+ days"), **Discount Anomalies** ("2 above rep average"), **Delivery Slippage** ("3 promise dates at risk"). One table: **Deal, Issue, Flagged, Action** — rows like "Zenith Co / Idle 9 days / Aug 24 / Nudge sent", "Delta LLC / Discount 22% vs avg 8% / Aug 25 / Escalated to Manager". Footer: **Escalate** (red) and **Nudge Rep** (blue).

Phrase the Issue column as "22% vs avg 8%" — computed from the z-score but far more readable to a judge than the statistic itself.

Add two charts below the table if time allows: discount distribution by rep with outliers marked, and margin trend.

**Screen 15 — Admin / Reporting Dashboard**, labelled **(Optional)** in the mockup. Subtitle: "Sales trends, approval bottlenecks and platform usage." ## Standing engineering rules

- One pricing function. If a number appears in two places, it came from the same call.
- Every engine function returns its explanation alongside its result. If we can't explain a number on screen, a judge won't believe it.
- Every state change writes an audit event.
- Config is data. Zero business thresholds in Python or TypeScript.
- Money as `Decimal` in Python, integer minor units over the wire. Never float.
- The portal has its own schema, its own dependency, its own route tree.
- Timestamps in UTC, formatted at the edge.
